# Entrepreneur pack

Templates for founders and solo operators. Covers the decisions that set a venture's direction and the ones that quietly consume its runway, plus a pattern template and session log. A venture's artifacts show what you did. The vault carries what you were betting on, what you refused to do, and the condition you set for calling it off.

## Templates

| File | Use when |
|---|---|
| `decision.md` | Generic starting point for any venture decision |
| `decision-bet.md` | Committing resources to something that might not work |
| `decision-vendor.md` | Adopting, renewing, or dropping a paid service |
| `decision-hire.md` | Hiring, contracting, or deliberately deferring a role |
| `decision-strategy.md` | Positioning, who you serve, and what you will not do |
| `pattern.md` | A reusable play worth naming |
| `session.md` | What happened in a working session |

## Decision categories

**Bets** (`decision-bet.md`) are the heart of an entrepreneur's vault. A bet differs from an ordinary choice in two ways: it can turn out false, and it has an expiry. The template forces both: a prediction stated as a number and a date, and kill criteria written down now, while you are not yet invested. Sunk cost is much harder to argue with six months in than it is on the day you start, so the value of this template is entirely in filling in the kill criteria before you need them.

**Vendor decisions** (`decision-vendor.md`) matter more than their monthly price. What you are actually signing up for is a lock-in surface and an exit cost, and neither appears on the invoice. Worth a short note even for cheap tools, because the cost of leaving is what you will want to know later.

**Hiring decisions** (`decision-hire.md`) are worth recording even when the answer is no. The reasoning behind a deferral is the thing you will want when the same pressure comes back, and "we decided not to yet, because X" saves re-arguing it. Refer to people by role or a candidate code, never by name, because hiring notes record judgements about real people in a vault that gets committed, so names, resumes, and compensation specifics stay out of it.

**Strategy decisions** (`decision-strategy.md`) are the easiest to leave implicit and the most expensive to get wrong. The highest-value line in the template is what you will not do, because that is the part that erodes first: the market you said you'd ignore, the feature you said was out of scope, the customer you said you weren't for. The template also asks what has to be true, so you can notice when the ground under a position has moved.

## Naming conventions

Decisions: `YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md`: date-prefixed, kebab-case slug derived from the title.

Patterns: `slug.md`: no date prefix. Patterns are timeless; decisions are time-stamped.

Sessions: `YYYY-MM-DD.md`: one file per day, multiple session blocks per file if you work in more than one sitting.
